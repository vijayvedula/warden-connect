Twelve shots, one minute

A Kling shot list for the 58-second pitch. Twelve generations of five seconds each, one caption per shot, assembled to sixty. Every prompt is written to be generated cold — no shot depends on the one before it except through the start/end frames noted.

Read this before you spend a single generation. Generative video cannot render legible text. Every string this film depends on — warden/offer.toml, WC-4002, effective = surface ∩ scope ∩ policy — will come back as convincing gibberish, and it will look worse than no text at all because a viewer can tell it is trying.

So none of these prompts asks for text, and the negative prompt forbids it explicitly. Every legible word is overlaid afterwards, from the caption track at the bottom of this sheet. Kling supplies the atmosphere; the editor supplies the argument.

The visual bible

Paste this ahead of every shot prompt. Consistency across twelve independent generations comes from repeating the same material description, not from luck — and this palette is the film's own, so the overlaid captions will sit in it rather than on it.

Style preamble — prepend to every shot
Abstract technical cinematography. An immense black void, deep ink-blue-black, with fine particulate haze and volumetric depth. All light comes from thin luminous filaments, translucent planes and molten pools. Strictly three light colours: pale cyan, warm amber, pale green — with rare crimson reserved for refusal. Matte surfaces, never glossy or chrome. Slow deliberate camera. Shallow depth of field, anamorphic 35mm, subtle film grain. No text or symbols anywhere.
Negative prompt — paste into every shot
text, letters, words, numbers, typography, glyphs, subtitles, captions, watermark, logo, signage, UI, interface, HUD, dashboard, charts, people, faces, hands, bodies, cartoon, anime, glossy plastic, chrome, lens flare, rainbow colours, oversaturation, clutter, warping, morphing artifacts, flicker, duplicated objects

The shots

T2V generates from the prompt alone. I2V takes a start and end frame — screenshot those two moments from the canvas film at the timecodes given, and Kling animates between them. Use I2V where the geometry has to be right.

The caption track

Overlaid in the editor, not generated. Serif for the claim a person makes, monospace for the machine's own words — the same rule the canvas film uses, and the reason it reads as one argument rather than a slideshow.

Assembly

  1. Generate shots 1–12 at 5s. Expect to regenerate three or four; drift and duplicated objects are normal. Keep the seed of anything you like.
  2. Cut hard, never dissolve. The film's rhythm is a series of statements. A cross-fade between two abstract shots reads as indecision.
  3. Overlay the caption track. Fade each in over ~0.4s, hold, fade out. Lower third, centred, generous margins.
  4. Grade for one ground. Match black points across all twelve so the void is the same void. This is the single biggest thing separating "twelve clips" from "one film".
  5. Sound. One sustained low drone throughout; a single sub-bass hit on shot 3 (the freeze), shot 7 (the seal) and shot 11 (the revoke pulse). Silence for the last half-second. No music with a beat — the cuts are the rhythm.

Two ways to assemble, and they are different films. All-Kling with overlaid captions is the mood piece — best for the investor audience, because it is about the shape of the problem. Intercutting the canvas panels for shots 5, 6 and 9 keeps the actual algebra and the actual GitOps lanes on screen — better for architects, who will want to read them. Pick one and hold it; alternating between two visual languages looks like an accident.
