warden-connect · the ladder, drawn

Forty thousand engineers,
five jurisdictions,
one rung at a time

A control plane for agent connections cannot be introduced as a mandate — mandates get routed around. So here is the same ladder as the adoption note, but climbed inside a bank that has four regulators, six booking centres and no appetite for a big-bang migration: who climbs it, where each business chooses to stop, and what happens at 03:00 when the domains that never agreed on anything have to act as one.

Five rungs · five domains · one estate Aldgate Group is fictional. The mechanisms are not.

how to read the ink

Three colours, and each one means something

Nothing on these plates is coloured for decoration. If a line changes colour, the thing it represents has changed category.

brass

The mechanism. Pins, keys, signatures, contracts, the control plane. Anything warden-connect itself produces or holds.

teal

People and traffic. Humans, agents, tool calls that are permitted to proceed. Movement that is allowed.

alarm

Refusal and honesty. A denied call, a severed contract, a drifted pin — and, importantly, a mediator that could not confirm.

graphite

The estate as it already is. Racks, clouds, vaults, org boundaries. Everything that existed before any of this was deployed.

the bank

Aldgate Group, and the nine people who decide whether this works

A group holding company in London, banking licences in four more places, and the usual consequence: the domains do not report to each other, do not share a deployment pipeline, and do not answer to the same regulator. Any design that requires them to agree before anything ships will ship nothing.

So the interesting question is not how one team adopts this. It is what the estate looks like when five domains adopt different amounts of it, at different speeds — and whether that estate can still act as one bank in an incident.

act one

Rhea climbs

Five rungs. Each is a superset of the last, each is independently valuable, and each one is a legitimate place to stop forever. Rhea starts alone, on a laptop in Bengaluru, with permission from nobody.

Watch what it costs her at each step. If her total time ever goes up, the rung above it never gets built.

1minute one · no state, no server, no registration

Give a tool surface a name

Rhea · alone · her laptop · nobody informed

Aldgate's payments MCP server offers a handful of tools. The first thing warden-connect does is turn that manifest into a pin: canonical serialisation of exactly the contracted fields, then a hash. Nothing else in the response is in scope, and nothing in scope is out of it.

# a pure function. no config, no daemon, no permission. connect canon tools.json --kind mcp surface_digest sha256:230c1f4acb7bf… get_balance sha256:8a1e… list_transactions sha256:c40b…

2hour one · a directory on disk

Know what you actually have

Rhea → Tomas · a CI step, not a database

Register the agents and servers that already exist. State is an append-only log under --root plus an in-memory projection — a directory, not a database. There is nothing to provision, which is why Rhea can do it before Tomas has heard of it, and why Tomas can later put it in the pipeline template without migrating anything.

Nothing is enforced. What Aldgate gains is the thing no bank has: a list, with owners. Every lifecycle transition lands in a tamper-evident hash chain, so the list can prove it was not edited afterwards.

connect register server --endpoint https://payments.internal/mcp \ --owner human:iva --zone corp.payments --surface tools.json connect register agent --card recon-agent.json --owner human:rhea \ --zone retail.apac-ops connect entities connect posture --unattested # who cannot prove what they claim
get: the register + evidence cost: a CI step ↓ stop here: this is a real deployment
3day one · still entirely local, still just files

Turn an approval into an artifact

Rhea requests → Aiko signs · seconds, not weeks

The core loop. A request names both parties, the exact tools wanted, and why. Policy answers instantly whether it auto-approves — internal, read-only, low tier, and Rhea never waits. Everything else reaches Aiko, and an approval is a detached signature from a named human's key, not a row in a table an operator with database access could write.

The payoff is the exit code. 4 means the artifact is not trustworthy. 3 means you asked for more than the ceiling allows. 5 means the surface looks poisoned. Aldgate's CI can now fail on the difference without scraping stderr — and a contract that expires is a review that actually happens.

connect request --from agent:recon --to server:payments \ --tools get_balance,list_transactions --justify "nightly reconciliation" \ --ttl 30d --issuer-key issuer.pem --kid k1 req_7f3c → require_approval (write surface: none · callee tier 3) connect approve req_7f3c --by human:aiko --approver-key aiko.pem minted conn_1a9b → contract.jws exp +30d
get: signed, expiring permission cost: an issuer key ↓ stop here: enforcement still optional
4week one · the first rung that changes behaviour

Put the contract on the path

Tomas ships it in the base image · Rhea changes no config

connect-mediate is one process composing unmodified Warden core with a connect decorator. Rhea's MCP client points where it already pointed. No manifest edit, no second network hop, and — the part that gets it through change advisory — it starts in --observe, byte-identical to not having deployed it.

When it does turn on, two things change. tools/list returns only the contracted tools, so the wrong tool cannot be called by accident. And an uncontracted tools/call is refused with WC-4002 before the upstream is ever spoken to.

connect-mediate --upstream "python payments_server.py" \ --mediator-id warden:mediator:retail-apac \ --caller agent:recon --callee server:payments \ --contract contract.jws --issuer-pub issuer.pub --kid k1 \ --observe # record findings; deny nothing. start here.
get: deny-by-default cost: a sidecar ↓ stop here: no control plane needed
5month one · the estate, not the repo

Make it estate-wide, and make containment one verb

Halima authorises · Tomas operates · Dele uses it at 03:00

Run the control plane. Mediators pull their contract sets and acknowledge exactly what they applied — pull, not push, because a lost push is silent whereas a missed pull surfaces as ACK lag you can alert on. In a bank, the difference between those two failure modes is the difference between a control and a belief.

Then containment is one verb across the whole estate, under dual control. Every contract the party holds, inbound and outbound, revoked at every mediator, with per-mediator confirmation. Mediators that do not confirm are reported as unconfirmed and fail closed themselves — because a containment tool that assumes success manufactures false confidence, which at 03:00 is worse than no tool.

connect serve --listen 0.0.0.0:8443 --issuer-key issuer.pem --kid k1 connect-mediate … --contracts https://connect.aldgate.internal --token $T --refresh 5 1 contract(s) installed, set sha256:230c1f4acb7bf seq 8, acked connect quarantine agent:recon --reason "SIEM: anomalous egress" \ --approver human:dele --approver human:halima # dual control
get: sub-minute containment cost: a service to operate — top rung
the design rule, restated Every rung must either shrink a surface or shorten a wait. A rung that does neither will be removed by the organisation, whether or not you agree with them.

Notice what is not on this ladder: a migration, a mandate, or a step where Rhea's total time goes up. Rung 2 costs a CI step she does not perform by hand, traded against weeks of waiting she no longer does. The friction budget is net negative — and it has to be, or rung 4 never gets permission to change behaviour.

the ratchet

Inside every rung that touches traffic, a smaller gradient

And it only turns one way. Rungs 4 and 5 are not a switch; they are a wheel with a pawl.

mode = observe

Record, deny nothing

Every check runs, every finding is written to the evidence chain, behaviour is byte-identical to not having deployed it. This is where Aldgate discovers the eleven agent connections nobody had recorded.

mode = flag

Surface, still permit

Findings become alerts to named owners. Drift, ceiling approach and upcoming expiry reach a human. Nothing is blocked, so nothing breaks — but the organisation starts reacting to the signal.

mode = enforce

Refuse

Uncontracted calls are denied before the upstream is spoken to. Only reachable once the register is populated enough that denial is correct rather than merely loud.

The order is not a suggestion. An early build of the issuer hardcoded enforce mode. At rung 2 every party is Unattested — nobody has provenance verification deployed yet — so nothing could ever be minted. Enforcement was demanded before the machinery that satisfies it existed, and the tool failed on first use, for the correct reason, in a way that looked exactly like a bug. Mode is now a deployment property, which is what lets rung 2 exist at all.
act two

Five domains, five different answers, and none of them wrong

The ladder is one engineer's path. An estate has many running at once, and where a domain stops is decided less by risk appetite than by three facts about it: who owns the deployment manifest, what the regulator will permit on the network, and whether an enforcement chokepoint already exists.

So Aldgate does not roll this out. Five domains each configure it, and the configurations are genuinely different — different rungs, different topologies, different modes, control planes in different places. Pick one.

Read the row that enforces nothing. Group Risk runs the register and no data plane at all — truthful inventory, owners, pins, drift detection, an evidence chain, and zero behaviour change. That is a deliberate product decision, and connect posture says so loudly. Honest visibility beats fake enforcement, because nobody investigates something that looks fine.

act three

The three moments where the estate has to act as one bank

Configuring five domains differently is easy. The design only earns its place if the differences stop mattering exactly when it counts. There are three such moments, and each one is a plate.

One · A relationship that crosses a jurisdiction

A wealth agent in Singapore needs balances from the payments server in London. Two domains, two regulators, two control planes, and no shared approval chain. What makes it tractable is that a contract is a ceiling, never a grant — so the answer is not a negotiation, it is an intersection.

The two control planes federate at the trust-anchor level rather than merging. Singapore's issuer key never signs for London, and London's approver registry never governs Singapore — Halima owns the anchor lifecycle, which is a named human's job precisely because it is the one thing federation makes fragile.

Two · 03:00, and one agent has to disappear

SIEM flags anomalous egress from a retail agent that, over eight months, accumulated contracts into corporate payments and a partner fintech. Dele needs the reachable set and then needs it gone. The estate it has to reach is four different topologies that were never designed together.

connect quarantine agent:retail-recon --reason "SIEM: anomalous egress" \ --approver human:dele --approver human:halima contracts revoked 7 (4 inbound · 3 outbound) mediators confirmed 12 / 13 41s unconfirmed warden:mediator:wealth-zrh-air (air-gapped · fails closed locally) → next bundle refresh 06:00 CET · tracked as INC-4471 open item

That last line is the whole argument. A containment tool that reported 13 / 13 here would be lying, and the lie would be indistinguishable from success on every dashboard in the bank. Explicit non-confirmation is the feature, and it is why the air-gapped domain is allowed to exist without weakening the claim the other four make.

Three · The regulator asks about the fourteenth

Margit is asked what a specific agent was permitted to reach on a date four months ago. The honest answer today, in most banks, is a reconstruction from tickets, wiki pages and the memory of somebody who has since left. Here it is a query — and it spans all five domains despite their having nothing in common operationally.

the invariant

effective_surface = declared ∩ contract ∩ token ∩ policy ∀ layers L : removing L → narrower or equal. never wider. ∀ mediators M : unreachable(M) → reported unconfirmed, never assumed applied.

Every rung must shrink a surface or shorten a wait. Every domain must be able to stop where it needs to. And every control must be able to say, out loud, that it did nothing.

If those three hold, adoption does not need a mandate. It needs a first rung small enough to climb on a Tuesday afternoon in Bengaluru.

Aldgate Group, its entities and the nine people above are fictional, invented to give the five rungs and seven topologies somewhere concrete to land. Commands, exit codes, error codes and mechanisms are those of the shipped warden-connect binaries.
