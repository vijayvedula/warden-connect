Fifty-four markets,
one ladder,
climbed at different speeds

A control plane for agent connections cannot be introduced as a mandate — mandates get routed around. That is true in any bank. It is structurally true at Standard Chartered, where the estate spans 54 markets, the engineers sit in shared service centres licensed in none of them, and a rule that has to clear twenty regulators before it takes effect will not take effect.

So this is the same five-rung ladder, mapped onto a network bank: who climbs it, where each segment stops, and what the corridor between two markets does to a permission.

Nothing on these plates is coloured for decoration. If a line changes colour, the thing it represents has changed category.

The mechanism. Pins, keys, signatures, contracts, the control plane. Anything warden-connect itself produces or holds.

teal

People and traffic. Humans, agents, tool calls that are permitted to proceed. Movement that is allowed.

alarm

Refusal and honesty. A denied call, a severed contract, a drifted pin — and, importantly, a mediator that could not confirm.

graphite

The estate as it already is. Racks, clouds, vaults, org boundaries. Everything that existed before any of this was deployed.

the shape of the estate

Four public facts that decide the whole topology

Standard Chartered is not a large domestic bank. It is a network bank, and four things about its published shape determine what a control plane for agent connections can look like before a single design decision is taken.

One · 54 markets, and the licence is per market

The group links over 50 markets across Asia, Africa and the Middle East from a London headquarters. Every one of those markets is a separate supervisory relationship. There is no single regulator to satisfy and therefore no single approval that unblocks the estate — which is exactly why a mandate is not an available instrument.

Two · The engineers are not in the markets they build for

Global Business Services centres in India, China and Malaysia employ around 19,000 people, with a new Bengaluru centre at RMZ Ecoworld. This is the fact that makes the design interesting: the person who writes the agent sits in one jurisdiction, the service it calls is licensed in another, and the client whose data moves is in a third. A connection is a corridor, not a link.

Three · The substrate already spans everything

The group has been running a software-defined private cloud on VMware Cloud Foundation across its 54 markets, with roughly 70% of global infrastructure already on it, alongside a multi-cloud path for core banking and trading. That is unusually good news here: contract distribution needs a substrate that reaches every mediator, and one already exists.

Four · One segment sells infrastructure to other banks

Under Ventures, Zodia Solutions was separated out to sell bank-grade digital-asset infrastructure to other financial institutions, while Zodia Custody's regulated custody activity folds into Corporate & Investment Banking. So cross-organisation trust is not a hypothetical edge case to be handled later — for one segment it is the product.

what follows from all four Federation is not the advanced topology you graduate to. At a network bank it is the starting topology, and a single global control plane is the configuration that cannot be made legal.

the friction budget
act one

One engineer, in Bengaluru, climbs

Five rungs. Each is a superset of the last, each is independently valuable, and each one is a legitimate place to stop forever. The climb starts alone, on a laptop in a shared service centre, with permission from nobody.

The agent reads balances from a transaction banking service licensed in Hong Kong, for a client relationship booked in Singapore. That is three jurisdictions before anything has been deployed — and it is the ordinary case here, not the awkward one.

Watch what each step costs. If the engineer's total time ever goes up, the rung above it never gets built.

1minute one · no state, no server, no registration

Give a tool surface a name

GBS agent engineer · Bengaluru · alone · nobody informed

The transaction banking service offers a handful of tools. The first thing warden-connect does is turn that manifest into a pin: canonical serialisation of exactly the contracted fields, then a hash. Nothing else in the response is in scope, and nothing in scope is out of it.

Note what this rung does not require: no network path to a control plane, no in-country deployment, no data leaving anywhere. It is a hash of a manifest, computed locally. That is why it is reachable in a market where nothing else is yet.

# a pure function. no config, no daemon, no permission. connect canon tools.json --kind mcp surface_digest sha256:230c1f4acb7bf… get_balance sha256:8a1e… list_transactions sha256:c40b…
get: change detection cost: one command ↓ stop here: still useful
2hour one · a directory on disk

Know what you actually have

Agent engineer → GBS platform · a CI step, not a database

Register the agents and services that already exist. State is an append-only log under --root plus an in-memory projection — a directory, not a database. There is nothing to provision, which is why one engineer can do it before platform engineering has heard of it, and why platform engineering can later put it in the pipeline template without migrating anything.

Nothing is enforced. What the group gains is the thing no network bank has: a list, with owners, per market. Every lifecycle transition lands in a tamper-evident hash chain, so the list can prove it was not edited afterwards — and because the log is a directory, it can sit inside a market that does not permit its records to leave.

connect register server --endpoint https://txb.hk.internal/mcp \ --owner team:txb-hk --zone hk.cib.txb --surface tools.json connect register agent --card recon-agent.json --owner team:gbs-blr \ --zone in.gbs.cib-ops connect entities connect posture --unattested # who cannot prove what they claim
get: the register + evidence cost: a CI step ↓ stop here: this is a real deployment
3day one · still entirely local, still just files

Turn an approval into an artifact

Engineer requests → CIB security architecture, Singapore signs · seconds, not weeks

The core loop. A request names both parties, the exact tools wanted, and why. Policy answers instantly whether it auto-approves — internal, read-only, low tier, and the engineer never waits. Everything else reaches an approver, and an approval is a detached signature from a named human's key, not a row in a table an operator with database access could write.

The contract also carries the zone of each party, which is what makes the corridor legible: this request is Bengaluru-built, Hong-Kong-served, Singapore-booked, and the artifact says so. A reviewer in Singapore can see the crossing without asking anyone what the agent is.

The payoff is the exit code. 4 means the artifact is not trustworthy. 3 means you asked for more than the ceiling allows. 5 means the surface looks poisoned. CI can now fail on the difference without scraping stderr — and a contract that expires is a review that actually happens.

connect request --from agent:recon --to server:txb-hk \ --tools get_balance,list_transactions --justify "nightly reconciliation" \ --ttl 30d --issuer-key hk-issuer.pem --kid hk-k1 req_7f3c → require_approval (crosses in.gbs → hk.cib · callee tier 1) connect approve req_7f3c --by role:cib-secarch-sg --approver-key sg.pem minted conn_1a9b → contract.jws exp +30d
get: signed, expiring permission cost: an issuer key ↓ stop here: enforcement still optional
4week one · the first rung that changes behaviour

Put the contract on the path

GBS platform ships it in the base image · the engineer changes no config

connect-mediate is one process composing unmodified Warden core with a connect decorator. The MCP client points where it already pointed. No manifest edit, no second network hop, and — the part that gets it through twenty change advisory boards — it starts in --observe, byte-identical to not having deployed it.

This is the rung the private cloud pays for. A base image that already reaches 54 markets means the sidecar arrives everywhere without a per-market deployment project, which is otherwise the point at which a rollout across a network bank stops.

When it does turn on, two things change. tools/list returns only the contracted tools, so the wrong tool cannot be called by accident. And an uncontracted tools/call is refused with WC-4002 before the upstream is ever spoken to — which matters more when the upstream is in another jurisdiction, because the refused call never becomes a cross-border data event at all.

connect-mediate --upstream "python txb_server.py" \ --mediator-id warden:mediator:hk.cib.txb.01 --issuer-id https://connect.hk.internal \ --caller agent:recon --callee server:txb-hk \ --contract contract.jws --issuer-pub hk-issuer.pub --kid hk-k1 \ --observe # record findings; deny nothing. start here.
get: deny-by-default cost: a sidecar ↓ stop here: no control plane needed
5month one · the estate, not the repo

Make it estate-wide, and make containment one verb

Group CISO authorises · GBS platform operates · Group SOC uses it at 03:00

Run the control plane — or rather, run several. This is where a network bank departs from the generic ladder: a single global control plane would hold contract metadata naming parties in markets whose regulators do not permit that metadata to leave. Rung 5 here means federated by construction, with a group trust anchor and per-market issuers.

Mediators pull their contract sets and acknowledge exactly what they applied — pull, not push, because a lost push is silent whereas a missed pull surfaces as ACK lag you can alert on. Across 54 markets on links of varying quality, that difference is the difference between a control and a belief.

Then containment is one verb across the whole estate, under dual control. Every contract the party holds, inbound and outbound, revoked at every mediator in every market, with per-mediator confirmation. Mediators that do not confirm are reported as unconfirmed and fail closed themselves — because a containment tool that assumes success manufactures false confidence, and at 03:00 across fifty-four markets that is worse than no tool.

connect serve --listen 0.0.0.0:8443 --issuer-key hk-issuer.pem --kid hk-k1 # one per market connect-mediate … --contracts https://connect.hk.internal --token $T --refresh 5 1 contract(s) installed, set sha256:230c1f4acb7bf seq 8, acked connect quarantine agent:recon --reason "SIEM: anomalous egress" \ --approver role:group-soc --approver role:group-ciso # dual control
get: sub-minute containment cost: a service to operate — top rung
the design rule, restated Every rung must either shrink a surface or shorten a wait. A rung that does neither will be removed by the organisation, whether or not you agree with them.

Notice what is not on this ladder: a migration, a mandate, or a step where the engineer's total time goes up. Rung 2 costs a CI step nobody performs by hand, traded against weeks of waiting nobody does any more. The friction budget is net negative — and it has to be, or rung 4 never gets permission to change behaviour in even one market, let alone fifty-four.

the ratchet

Inside every rung that touches traffic, a smaller gradient

And it only turns one way. Rungs 4 and 5 are not a switch; they are a wheel with a pawl.

mode = observe

Record, deny nothing

Every check runs, every finding is written to the evidence chain, behaviour is byte-identical to not having deployed it. This is where a market discovers the agent connections into it that nobody had recorded.

mode = flag

Surface, still permit

Findings become alerts to named owners. Drift, ceiling approach and upcoming expiry reach a human. Nothing is blocked, so nothing breaks — but the organisation starts reacting to the signal.

mode = enforce

Refuse

Uncontracted calls are denied before the upstream is spoken to. Only reachable once the register is populated enough that denial is correct rather than merely loud.

The order is not a suggestion. An early build of the issuer hardcoded enforce mode. At rung 2 every party is Unattested — nobody has provenance verification deployed yet — so nothing could ever be minted. Enforcement was demanded before the machinery that satisfies it existed, and the tool failed on first use, for the correct reason, in a way that looked exactly like a bug. Mode is now a deployment property, which is what lets rung 2 exist at all.
act two

Five segments, five different answers, and none of them wrong

The ladder is one engineer's path. A group runs many at once, and where a segment stops is decided less by risk appetite than by three facts about it: who owns the deployment manifest, what the regulator will permit to cross a border, and whether an enforcement chokepoint already exists.

So the group does not roll this out. Five segments each configure it — Corporate & Investment Banking, Wealth & Retail Banking, Ventures, GBS & Technology, and Group Functions — and the configurations are genuinely different: different rungs, different topologies, different modes, control planes in different countries. Pick one.

Read the row that enforces nothing. Group Functions runs the register and no data plane at all — truthful inventory, owners, pins, drift detection, an evidence chain, and zero behaviour change. That is a deliberate choice, and connect posture says so loudly. Honest visibility beats fake enforcement, because nobody investigates something that looks fine. It matters more here than at a domestic bank: second-line assurance that also operated a chokepoint in 54 markets would be assuring its own control.
the combination

Build one yourself, and find out where it breaks

Rung, topology and mode are three independent dials. At a network bank there is a fourth — where the regulator permits the record of a permission to sit — and it is the one that quietly decides the other three.

Most combinations are reachable. Some are incoherent, and those are the interesting part: nearly all of them are configurations that look deployed, report success, and either enforce nothing or store something in the wrong country.

Three of those combinations are worth dwelling on, because all three are the same species of failure. Control-plane-only in enforce is a system with nothing on the path being told to deny. Rung 2 in enforce is a system being told to require attestation before anything can attest — a real regression, found by running the binary rather than by the test suite.

The third is the one this bank owns. Rung 5, in-country residency, any topology that centralises: every check fires, every uncontracted call is refused, every dashboard is green — and the contract set naming in-country parties is now replicated to a control plane outside the market. The control did its job. The deployment of the control was the breach. None of the three errors in a way that reads as a configuration mistake.

the question to ask on every rung What would this look like if the control silently did nothing? Prefer the negative assertion — the upstream never executed it; nothing was appended; the string is absent from the bytes.

act three

The three moments where 54 markets have to behave like one bank

Configuring five segments differently is easy. The design only earns its place if the differences stop mattering exactly when it counts. There are three such moments here, and the first one is not in the generic version of this argument at all.

One · The corridor, and a fifth ceiling

An agent built in Bengaluru wants balances from a transaction banking service licensed in Hong Kong, for a relationship booked in Singapore. Three jurisdictions, one call. The generic model has four narrowing layers — declared surface, contract surface, token scope, policy decision. A network bank has a fifth, and it is the one that can invalidate the other four.

The two control planes peer at the trust-anchor level rather than merging. The Hong Kong issuer never signs for Singapore, and no approver registry governs a zone it does not sit in — anchor lifecycle is a named function's job precisely because it is the one thing federation makes fragile.

Why residency has to be a ceiling and not a policy. If it were a policy check, it would live in the action boundary and be evaluated per call — correct, but it would say nothing about where the contract sits. Modelling it as a narrowing layer forces the question at mint time: this artifact names an in-country party, so which control plane is even allowed to hold it?

Two · 03:00, and one agent has to disappear from every market it reached

SIEM flags anomalous egress from a GBS-built agent that, over eight months, accumulated contracts into transaction banking in three markets, a wealth service under in-country residency, and a Ventures counterparty outside the group. The incident analyst needs the reachable set and then needs it gone. The estate it has to reach was never designed together.

connect quarantine agent:gbs-recon --reason "SIEM: anomalous egress" \ --approver role:group-soc --approver role:group-ciso contracts revoked 9 (5 inbound · 4 outbound · 4 zones) mediators confirmed 21 / 23 47s unconfirmed warden:mediator:ame.lagos.01 (bundle cycle · fails closed locally) not ours to revoke warden:peer:zodia-counterparty (notified · their control plane acts) → next bundle refresh 06:00 WAT · counterparty ack pending · both tracked open

Those last two lines are the whole argument. A containment tool reporting 23 / 23 here would be lying twice: once about a market it could not reach, and once about an organisation it has no authority over. Explicit non-confirmation is the feature — and it is why a thin market and a cross-organisation counterparty are both allowed to exist without weakening the claim the other twenty-one make.

Three · Two regulators, one agent, and neither answer may contain the other

The HKMA asks what a given agent was permitted to reach in Hong Kong on a date four months ago. Within the same fortnight, the RBI asks the equivalent question about India. In most banks both answers are reconstructions. Here both are queries — and the interesting requirement is not that they can be answered, but that neither export may contain the other market's records.

connect export --as-of 2026-04-14T00:00Z --party agent:gbs-recon --zone hk.cib 3 contracts effective · 1 expired since · 0 revoked chain verified head sha256:9c2e… checkpoints 41 signed ✓ scope: hk.cib only — 0 records from any other zone are present in this export

That last line is assertable rather than promised, because the chain it verifies is the in-country chain and there is no other chain behind it. The negative assertion is the one worth making: not we filtered out the other markets, but the other markets were never in this store.

the invariant

effective_surface = declared ∩ contract ∩ token ∩ policy ∩ residency ∀ layers L : removing L → narrower or equal. never wider. ∀ mediators M : unreachable(M) → reported unconfirmed, never assumed applied. ∀ zones Z : record(Z) stays in Z. anchors federate. stores do not.

Every rung must shrink a surface or shorten a wait. Every market must be able to stop where its regulator requires. And every control must be able to say, out loud, that it did nothing.

If those three hold, adoption does not need a mandate — which is fortunate, because across 54 markets and twenty supervisors, a mandate was never an instrument anyone actually had. It needs a first rung small enough to climb on a Tuesday afternoon in Bengaluru.
