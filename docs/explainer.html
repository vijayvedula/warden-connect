warden-connect

connection control plane for agents

An agent invokes a tool. Funds move, records are read, messages go out. Authentication answers who is calling. Authorization answers what they may do. Neither answers the third question:

Who agreed to this, and can you show me?

02 · model

Effective Permissions

Two policies

The word does double duty here, and conflating the two is the usual confusion.

PolicyComponentAsksRuns
connect-policy.tomlwarden-connectmay this contract exist — who approves it, what TTL, which zone crossingsonce, at issuance
warden policywardenmay this call proceed, given the token and the contextevery call

warden-connect never sees the session token and never makes the per-call decision. It decides which capabilities are reachable at all, and hands that ceiling to warden.

02 · model

Effective Permissions

The family

ComponentContributesApplied

warden-delegateattenuation inside terms.delegation — may reduce depth or scope, never raise eitherper onward hop

The interface between them is deliberately small: two artifacts and one identifier — the session token, the connection contract, and cid.

And it runs both ways. Denied-action patterns and ceiling breaches from warden feed posture scoring in connect: repeated denials degrade a party and shorten its re-attestation interval.

03 · authorization

Bilateral Consent

Most systems have only acceptance: somebody requested access and an administrator approved. The party being called was never asked.

ConsentGiven byRecorded asLives in

pre_grantedanyone in this audience may have itmints immediately — the offer is the consent

The provider writes terms about an audience, never a consumer: to = { zone = "internal.*" }. Writable on day one, before anyone has asked.

An offer only ever permits the asking.

05 · lifecycle

Contract Lifecycle

Dispositions

DispositionShown asMeansWho acts
GrantOKevery item is pre-grantednobody — it mints
NeedsApprovalPENDINGat least one item is gated; nothing mintedthe callee's registered owner
RefusedREFUSEDno term covers it for this consumera conversation about the audience

Two rules combine them: a refusal outranks a gate (never ask a provider to consent to something their offer cannot express), and one gated item holds the whole need (a partial contract is one the consumer did not ask for).

08 · why now

Agentic Runtime

A service calling an APIAn agent calling a tool
Who chose the calla developer, at build timea model, at run time
Reviewable before it happensyes — it is in the diffno
How it is configureddeployment configa JSON file somebody edited

What you can review instead—the capability, before the call exists

You cannot review the call. So you review the capability.

A contract is reviewed once, by the two parties who own the risk, and bounds every call afterwards — including the ones nobody predicted.
